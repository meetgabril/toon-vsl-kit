## Funnel vs loop
- **Funnel:** you pay for every new person. It ends at the sale.
- **Loop:** every member's action brings the next member. The output becomes the input.

## The 4 parts of every loop
1. **Trigger:** a moment worth sharing (a win, an event, a gift).
2. **Action:** the member invites, shares or hosts.
3. **Payoff:** both sides get something real.
4. **New member:** joins, hits the same trigger, and the loop turns again.

## Three loops that ran for decades
- **Tupperware, 1950s:** party guests were asked to host the next party. Hosts got merchandise as thanks.
- **Dropbox:** invite a friend, you both get extra space. In 2010 its founder said signups rose 60% for good.
- **Harley Owners Group, 1983:** a pin, a patch, a card, then rallies. Today: about 1,500 chapters.

## Four loops for your community
- **Win post:** a member shares a result, their followers ask how, and some join.
- **Bring-a-friend session:** once a month, every member may bring one guest.
- **Member host:** members run a session and invite their own people.
- **Two-sided welcome:** the inviter and the new member both get a bonus.

## Design yours in 5 questions
- [ ] What moment is worth sharing?
- [ ] What exactly does the member do?
- [ ] What does each side get?
- [ ] Where does the new person land first?
- [ ] How fast does it come back around?

:::warning Loop killers
Rewarding only the inviter. Inviting people into an empty room. Asking before the first win. More than two steps to invite.
:::

## Measure it
- **Invites per member** × **share who join** = new members each member brings.
- Above 1, the loop feeds itself. Below 1, it still lowers the cost of every member.
